# Question 5 — Scope

**Task.** Interactive Python interface investigating electronic properties of a semiconductor
crystal via Fermi–Dirac statistics. 10 marks. **Not started** — question 3 comes first.

## Required deliverables (from the PDF)
1. Explain Fermi–Dirac statistics, Fermi energy, density of states, and their significance.
2. Interface accepting temperature $T$, carrier concentration $n$, effective mass $m^*$,
   band-gap $E_g$, and an energy range.
3. Compute $E_F$, $k_F$, $v_F$, $T_F$ with the equations stated.
4. Plot conduction band, valence band, Fermi level, and $f(E)$ versus energy.
5. Compute and plot $D(E)$ and the occupied density of states $D(E)f(E)$.
6. $E_F$ versus temperature, numerically determined, with explanation.
7. $E_F$ versus carrier concentration, with discussion.
8. Demonstration with suitable inputs, commented code, interpretation, references.

## Shared with question 3
Same module split, same validate-against-a-limit habit, same figure discipline — inherited as
convention, not as imported code. The only shared file is `common/style.py`. The physics link
worth one cross-reference in each report: both questions are periodic-system band problems that
end in a density of states, and both gaps come from a two-member basis splitting one band in two
(mass contrast in Q3, periodic potential in Q5).

Numerics differ and the test files won't resemble each other: Q3 is an eigenvalue problem,
Q5 is a root-find for E_F with a bracketing step that can fail at low T.

## Notes carried forward from question 3
Same structure applies: physics core separate from UI, closed-form validation checks before any
reported number, figures generated by script. The natural check here is that the numerically
solved $E_F$ reproduces the Boltzmann (non-degenerate) approximation
$n = N_c \exp[-(E_c - E_F)/k_BT]$ in the dilute limit, and the $T \to 0$ degenerate limit
$E_F = \hbar^2 (3\pi^2 n)^{2/3} / 2m^*$.
